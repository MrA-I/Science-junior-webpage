Retina: the layer at the back of the eye that detects light.
Rods: light-sensitive cells that work in dim light and detect shades of grey.
Cones: light-sensitive cells that detect colour in bright light.
Short-sighted: distant objects look blurry, because light focuses in front of the retina.
Long-sighted: near objects look blurry, because light would focus behind the retina.

Go further: eye conditions and technology

Tap each card to read more.

Short and long sight. If the eyeball is too long or the lens bends light too strongly, the image forms in front of the retina (short sight). If the eye is too short, or the lens is too weak, the image would form behind it (long sight). Glasses add a lens that fixes where the light focuses.
Colour and blue light. We see a red apple because it reflects red light and absorbs most other colours. Some screen filters block a percentage of blue light that reaches the eye, which may reduce eye strain.
Modern vision technology. Contact lenses work like glasses but sit on the eye. Laser eye surgery reshapes the cornea so it bends light correctly. Both rely on the science of refraction.

Eye conditions and corrective lenses

Research myopia (short sight) or hyperopia (long sight) and create a presentation that:

  • describes how the eye responds to light in your chosen condition
  • explains how a concave or convex lens corrects it, with a diagram
  • highlights how technology such as spectacles helps people with vision impairments

Support: answer guiding questions: What part is not focusing correctly? Does the lens need to spread light out or bring it together? Extend: analyse how contact lenses and laser eye surgery use science to improve or restore vision.
