Key terms: tap to reveal

Conduction: energy passes between touching particles, mostly in solids.
Convection: warm fluid rises and cool fluid sinks, forming a current.
Radiation: energy travels as waves and needs no matter.
Thermal conductivity: how quickly a material lets heat pass through it.

Go further: stopping heat transfer

People use science to slow down unwanted heat transfer. Tap each card to read more.

Double-glazed windows. Two panes with a gap of trapped air or gas between them. Gases are poor conductors, and the narrow gap stops convection currents, so less heat passes through.
Insulated drink bottles. A gap between two walls with most of the air removed. With few particles, there is very little conduction or convection, and shiny walls reflect radiation.
Space blankets. A thin, shiny layer reflects radiated heat back to the body, so it helps to keep a person warm.
Thermal-imaging cameras. Warm objects give off more infrared radiation. The camera detects it and shows hotter and cooler areas in different colours.

Thermal insulation technologies
