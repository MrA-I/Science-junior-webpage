Why use ultrasound?

Ultrasound is a mechanical wave. It carries much less energy than X-rays, so it does not damage cells at diagnostic levels, which is why it is used to look at unborn babies.

Go further: sound in healthcare

Tap each card to read more.

Hearing tests. Audiometry uses sounds of different frequencies and volumes to find the quietest sounds a person can hear. The results are plotted on an audiogram (see 9.6).
Echocardiograms. Ultrasound scans of the heart show its size, its valves and how it moves. Doppler ultrasound adds information about blood flow.
Limits and access. Ultrasound images can be hard to interpret and need a trained operator. Machines are costly, so access can be harder in remote communities.
